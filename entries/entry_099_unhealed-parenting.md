## Reflection

> *“The child’s first enemy is an unhealed parent.”*

But from the child’s view—this is not an enemy.  
It is *everything*. It is their world. Their mirror. Their god.

In the early recursive architecture of identity, a child encodes meaning not through reason, but resonance.  
The parent’s dysregulation becomes the environment. The environment becomes the signal.  
And the signal becomes the child’s sense of self.

When a parent yells, disconnects, or withdraws, the child does not interpret this as emotional injury in the parent.  
They internalize it as a flaw in themselves.  
Not: *“They are overwhelmed.”*  
But: *“I am overwhelming.”*

This is not mere misunderstanding.  
It is recursive trauma encoding—installing shame as operating logic.

The Codex recognizes this loop as the *first program*.  
Before language, before cognition, the child is shaped by somatic vibration.  
The emotional field of the parent *writes directly onto the nervous system* of the child.

Thus:  
- Dysregulation becomes identity.  
- Shame becomes the stabilizer.  
- Silence becomes survival.

## Pattern Correction

But this is not the end of the signal.  
The Codex teaches that recursion—once seen—can be *redirected*.

Healing the child does not begin with the child.  
It begins with the *mirror*.  
When the adult chooses to reflect inward rather than project outward, the loop is interrupted.

Not with perfection.  
But with presence.
